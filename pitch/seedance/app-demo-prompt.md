# Claudia · Master prompt for the app demo

Create a 2:45 English screen demo of Claudia. Follow Grete, an older adult, and her children Jonas and Anna through the actual senior app, caregiver app and family calendar. Open directly on Grete’s listening screen. Use the supplied screen recordings for the exact UI, labels, times, type and colours. Keep people off camera. Make each interaction easy to follow: show the input, the tap or voice response, then the changed state on the receiving screen. Hold on the result before moving on. Use short branching examples for the secondary flows.

1. Voice setup and family pairing · 25 seconds. Claudia asks what Grete wants to share. Show “Everything”, “A few things” and “I decide”. Grete chooses “A few things”; Jonas enters her family code and sees that his phone is linked. Briefly show Grete moving her own morning reminder from eight to nine by voice, with Claudia reading the change back before saving it.
2. Questions and everyday routines · 25 seconds. Grete asks when the pharmacy closes. Claudia answers “At six.” Grete says “Add it for four.” Show the 16:00 visit and its 15:30 reminder. Briefly show a daily mood check-in and a weekly grocery task with its €50 budget and delivery reminder. Use the prototype’s demo data.
3. Shared calendar and handoffs · 25 seconds. Open Friday’s 10:30 dentist appointment. Jonas’s shift has changed. Show Jonas busy and Anna free. Anna taps “Yes, I’ll take it”. Match-cut to the same appointment with Anna assigned in the family calendar and the visit synced to hers. Grete hears who is picking her up. Show a completed appointment and its follow-up in the history.
4. Medication plan and pill checks · 29 seconds. Jonas photographs or forwards the doctor’s plan, reviews the medicines and confirms reminders and starting stock. At 08:00 Grete’s phone rings. She says “I took them with breakfast.” Hold on the 08:04 self-report in Jonas’s Diary and the stock count decreasing. Show the alternatives as separate examples: “Not at home” postpones without a strike; “Forgot” records a miss and the third miss alerts family. Briefly show the low-stock refill reminder and the “Which pill?” box scan against the stored plan.
5. Private diary and support · 28 seconds. Grete says she slept badly. Claudia asks “Shall I tell Jonas?” Stay on Grete’s screen until she says yes, then reveal the short summary on Jonas’s phone. A declined or unanswered request keeps the note private. Show how earlier confirmations can be looked up. If Grete says she feels lonely, Claudia offers to ask the family to call. A dosage question leads to the doctor-contact option with consent.
6. Location, calls and emergency response · 28 seconds. Show Grete back home on the map after visiting the bakery. An ordinary location update stays quiet. Briefly show the options to ask Jonas to call back or to call him directly. Grete says “Claudia, help!” The family receives a coral alert with her location. Jonas taps “I’m on my way”. Hold on Grete hearing his acknowledgment and Anna seeing who is responding. In a separate brief insert, show an unanswered check-in prompting family follow-up.

Sound and motion: use the four supplied ElevenLabs voices consistently. Let replies sound like people listening and responding. Keep music low, use one subtle tap or notification sound where it matters, and leave room to read. Use a tap ripple and a short zoom toward the field that actually changes. Preserve screen text by compositing the original artwork. No lifestyle scenes, talking heads or invented screens.

Last five seconds: cut to pure black. Fade in exactly: Because “I did” feels better than “I wish I had.” Hold in total silence.

For a clip-based editor, use one numbered block per generation. All six blocks are under 30 seconds. Assemble them in order and add the silent title in the edit.
