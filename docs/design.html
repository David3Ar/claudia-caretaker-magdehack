Principles

Three rules decide every screen. When two of them pull apart, the higher one wins.

  • Say it, don't find it

    Anything the app can do, you can ask for out loud. A screen shows what was said and what happens next, never a menu to dig through.

  • Buttons for what can't wait

    Calling family and calling for help are always one large press away, on every senior screen, in the same place.

  • Everyday words only

    Tablets, not medication adherence. "Jonas is at work", not a location status. No settings page for the senior at all.

  • Caregivers get the detail

    The family can type, tap and plan. Their app and the dashboard add forms and history, in the same calm look.

Colour

White, black and one soft grey do the work. Colour appears only when it means something.

Six colours

Contrast is kept high throughout. Text on lime, amber and coral is always black.

  • Paper#ffffffEvery screen background
  • Mist#edf1efDashboard backdrop, form fields, quiet tags
  • Ink#000000Text, main buttons, the voice screen
  • Lime#c6f432All good, done, confirmed
  • Amber#ffb23eLate, unanswered, needs someone
  • Coral#ff5a3cEmergency, and nothing else

One scale, used everywhere

The same three steps colour a tablet slot, a calendar entry and an alert, so nobody has to learn a second code. The halo around the voice orb is this scale, blended.

Type

One typeface made for tired eyes, and a second voice for the words Claudia speaks.

Atkinson Hyperlegible Next

Drawn by the Braille Institute so that similar letters stay apart: I, l and 1, or O and 0. Used for everything you read or press.

Aa Il1 O0 Ää Öß 8:04

Newsreader Italic

Reserved for speech. If it is in this italic, Claudia said it or is about to. It never labels a button.

Shall I remind you again?

Voice

The orb is Claudia. It shows, without a word of text, whether she is waiting, hearing you, thinking or talking.

Live voice mode

Full screen and black, so the only thing to look at is the orb. Try the four states.

Jonas is at work. Shall I ask him to call you?

  • WaitingHalo turns slowly. Say "Claudia" or touch the orb.
  • ListeningHalo widens and breathes. Your words appear in plain type.
  • ThinkingHalo spins fast, dots hop. Never longer than a breath.
  • SpeakingBars move with her voice. Her words appear in italic.

Calendar

One calendar for the whole family. Grete hears it, the family sees it, and anything unsettled is asked as a single question.
