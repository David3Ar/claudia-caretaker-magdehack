CLAUDIA - COMPETITOR ANALYSIS
Magdehack, Pilot #6 (mental health of family caregivers)
Researched on the web on 2026-11-14. Prices and features change, so check the
vendor pages before quoting a number in the pitch.

==============================================================================
1. THE PROBLEM WE ARE SOLVING
==============================================================================

Germany, end of 2023 (Destatis care statistics):
- About 86 % of people in need of care, around 4.9 million, are cared for at home.
- About 3.1 million of them get only Pflegegeld, so they are cared for mainly by
  relatives, with no care service involved.

The relatives (Jonas, Anna) carry the worry: Did Mum take her pills? Where is
she? Is she OK right now? Today they find out by calling over and over, or they
buy several separate products that don't talk to each other.

Claudia is one voice agent for the senior (Grete) plus one app for the family.
It covers the calendar, the pill checker, live location with emergency alerts,
and Q&A/setup.

==============================================================================
2. COMPETITORS
==============================================================================

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.1 Hausnotruf (DRK, Johanniter, Malteser, ASB, private providers)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What it is: a button worn on the wrist or neck, a base station, and a 24/7
call centre. It is the standard emergency solution in Germany.
Cost: about 23-39 EUR per month base fee for self-payers (Stiftung Warentest),
plus up to 60 EUR to connect. GPS, a fall sensor or an "all OK" button cost
extra. The Pflegekasse only pays if the person has a Pflegegrad.

Problems:
- It only does emergencies. No medication, no appointments, no daily
  well-being, and no picture of the day for the family.
- It depends on a button. If Grete doesn't wear it, or can't reach it, nothing
  happens. Voice ("help") is not the main way to trigger it.
- The basic version only works at home. Mobile/GPS costs extra.
- The family is the last to hear. The call centre calls the contact person, and
  the relatives get no live view.
- Testers warned about unfavourable contract terms from some private providers.

Why Claudia is better:
- Grete can raise an alarm by voice: "Hey Claudia, help!" A button is only the
  fallback. Every caregiver gets a full-screen alert with her location at once
  (EMG-1..7).
- If Grete doesn't answer, the status escalates on its own: amber, then coral.
- The same system also runs pills, the calendar and day-to-day questions, so
  it is used every day and not only in an emergency.
- Positioning: Claudia complements Hausnotruf, it doesn't replace it. It can
  run next to an existing contract, and a professional call centre could be
  added later.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.2 ElliQ (Intuition Robotics, USA)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What it is: a tabletop "companion robot" with a screen that talks, reminds and
suggests activities. Families get the free ElliQ Connect app.
Cost: leased only. 249 USD setup fee plus a monthly membership (reports say
about 30 USD/month on an annual plan). 1,500 USD fee if the device isn't
returned after you cancel.

Problems:
- US only and English only. It isn't available in Germany.
- It is a fixed device in one room. It doesn't help when Grete is out, so no
  location and no emergency outside the home.
- Reviews call the price high. One reviewer found the experience "somewhat
  depressing", and the vendor says it isn't designed for severe cognitive
  impairment.
- The family side is thin: suggested reminders that Grete must approve, a
  "last active" time, and alerts only if the senior turns them on. The family
  doesn't see whether a pill was actually taken.

Why Claudia is better:
- It runs on a phone Grete already carries, works in German, and has no
  hardware lease.
- It works at home and outside: live location, "Where is Grete?", and help
  anywhere.
- The family view is built around what they actually worry about: pill status
  (Taken / Not at home / Forgot, 3-strike counter), the next appointment, and
  where Grete is now.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.3 Amazon Alexa Together / Alexa Emergency Assist
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What it was: a remote-caregiving subscription on Echo devices (19.99 USD per
month). It had an activity feed for caregivers, 24/7 urgent response and up to
10 caregivers.
Status: Amazon's own page says that since May 2025 Alexa Together is no longer
available. It points users to "Alexa Emergency Assist".

Problems:
- It was discontinued. Families who relied on it lost it, which shows the risk
  of a side product inside a big ecosystem.
- It was only for the US and needed Echo speakers in the home, so it did
  nothing outside the house.
- It was general-purpose Alexa. There was no structured medication check and
  no escalation rules made for caregivers.

Why Claudia is better:
- Caregiving is the whole product, not a side feature.
- It is phone-based, so it goes wherever Grete goes.
- The flows are made for this use: a 3-option pill check, strike counter,
  low-stock alert to the doctor, and a call request ("Ask him to call me").

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.4 Medication apps (MyTherapy, Medisafe and others)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What they are: smartphone pill reminders with a dose diary. Medisafe adds a
"Medfriend" who is told about missed doses.
Cost: free tiers. Some caregiver features are behind a paywall (sources
disagree).

Problems:
- The senior must type in the plan and tap "done". Many seniors dismiss
  notifications, and a tap without taking the pill, or a pill without the tap,
  creates a false record.
- They only cover medication: no location, no emergency, no calendar.
- Reviews call the interfaces feature-heavy and overwhelming for older users.

Why Claudia is better:
- Set-up without typing: the family sends the plan by email or WhatsApp or as a
  photo, or Grete just tells Claudia (PILL-1..10).
- Claudia rings at the scheduled time and asks out loud. Grete answers by voice
  with "taken", "not at home" or "forgot", so there is nothing to tap.
- It doesn't just say "missed". "Not at home" and "Forgot" are different cases.
  Missed doses count up to 3 strikes before the family is alerted, which means
  fewer false alarms.
- It warns about low stock, and if Grete isn't sure which pill is which, she
  can point the camera at it and Claudia identifies it.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.5 Location apps (Life360, Apple Find My, Google Find My Device)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What they are: family location sharing on the phone. Life360 adds place
alerts, crash detection and SOS in paid plans.

Problems:
- They are a map and nothing else. They don't know whether Grete is OK, only
  where her phone is.
- Privacy: in 2021 The Markup reported that Life360 sold precise location
  data. The company has since said it stopped, but trust is damaged.
- Constant tracking feels like surveillance, and seniors often resist it. The
  apps notify about every place change, which adds to caregiver stress instead
  of reducing it.
- They are built for parents watching children. There is no senior flow and no
  voice.

Why Claudia is better:
- It answers in words, not only on a map: "Where is Grete?" gets "At home since
  10:32". Normal movement sends no push (LOC-7), so the family is told about
  real problems, not every walk to the bakery.
- Sharing works both ways and with consent. Grete can ask "Where is my son?"
  and gets a coarse answer like "at work". What is shared can be set in
  settings (LOC-8).
- Location is tied to the emergency flow, so an alert always carries her
  position.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.6 Senior phones (Doro + "Response by Doro")
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What it is: phones with big buttons, an assistance button that sends an alarm
and GPS position to "Responders" (family) in a free app, and remote help with
settings. A paid Premium tier adds an alarm centre.

Problems:
- You need special Doro hardware (only certain models).
- The alarm is a button. Daily support is limited to settings, and there is no
  medication check, calendar or conversation.
- The family only hears something when the button is pressed. There is no daily
  "all OK" picture.

Why Claudia is better:
- The app runs on a normal smartphone.
- It is voice-first and hands-free after "Hey Claudia", which is easier than
  finding a button under stress.
- It gives a daily picture (pills, appointments, place) and covers emergencies.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.7 Smartwatches with fall detection (Apple Watch etc.)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Problems:
- It only works if the watch is worn and charged every day, which many seniors
  struggle with. A loose band can stop it from being detected as worn.
- Users report missed "soft" falls and false alarms from shaking or gestures.
  Apple says high activity can trigger it, and repeated false alarms make
  people switch the feature off.
- It only alerts. It doesn't support daily life or show the family a view of
  the day.

Claudia's position:
- A smartwatch is an optional extra alert source in our spec ("Could"), not the
  backbone. Voice, the not-responding escalation and the button cover the gap
  when no watch is worn.

------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.8 Apps for caregivers (Nui Care, DAK Pflege-App, ADAC Pflegeassistent)
------------------------------------------------------------------------------
What they are: German apps for pflegende Angehörige (family carers). They offer
care guides, help with Pflegekasse benefits and applications, checklists,
expert chat and organising a care team. Nui Care powers the DAK app and runs
partnerships with AOK Bayern, Allianz PKV and ADAC.

Problems:
- They help with paperwork and knowledge, not with the live situation. They
  can't tell Jonas whether his mother took her pills or where she is right now.
- They are for the caregiver only. The senior has no part in them.

Why Claudia is better:
- It connects both sides. Grete talks to Claudia, and Jonas and Anna see the
  result.
- It reduces the daily worry directly, which is the core of Pilot #6 (caregiver
  mental health).
- Opportunity: these apps are partners rather than rivals. Claudia's data
  (adherence, appointments) could feed their care-organisation features, and
  the health insurers already pay for this category.

4. OUR EDGE IN ONE SENTENCE
==============================================================================

Competitors each fix one piece: an alarm button, a pill reminder, a map, or a
guide. Claudia is a single hands-free voice companion that Grete simply talks
to, and it gives Jonas and Anna a calm, honest picture of her day, with alerts
only when it matters.

==============================================================================
5. QUESTIONS THE JURY MAY ASK (be ready)
==============================================================================

- "Why not just Hausnotruf?" It is emergency-only, button-based and limited to
  the home. Claudia complements it, and a call centre can be added later.
- "Privacy / surveillance?" Sharing has consent and is coarse for G -> C.
  Normal movement sends no push, and sharing is set in settings. The open point
  is wake-word privacy and battery use.
- "Does it work for dementia?" It is designed for mild to moderate memory
  issues. Severe impairment needs professional care, which is also ElliQ's
  limit.
- "Business model?" The Pflegekasse and health insurers already pay for
  Hausnotruf and caregiver apps (Nui/DAK), so the B2B2C route through insurers
  is realistic.
- Our weak spots to be honest about: there is no 24/7 professional call centre
  yet, it relies on Grete's phone being charged and nearby, and wake-word
  support in ElevenLabs still needs checking.
